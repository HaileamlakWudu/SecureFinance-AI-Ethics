# SecureFinance Africa – AI Ethics, Governance & Legal Compliance Assessment

## 1. Project Overview

This repository contains an AI ethics, governance, risk management, and legal compliance assessment of a fictional financial technology company called **SecureFinance Africa**.

The company proposes to use artificial intelligence (AI) in three major areas:

1. **Identity Verification:** Using identity documents and biometric verification to confirm customer identities.
2. **Fraud Detection:** Analysing financial transactions to identify potentially fraudulent activities.
3. **Financial Risk Assessment:** Evaluating customer creditworthiness and supporting automated financial decisions.

The assessment examines the ethical, technical, operational, and legal risks associated with these proposed AI systems, with Nigeria as the initial deployment context and potential international expansion considered.

## 2. Project Objectives

The main objectives are to:

- Identify ethical risks associated with AI-powered financial services.
- Apply the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0).
- Develop a structured AI risk register.
- Assess relevant legal and regulatory requirements.
- Evaluate fairness, explainability, and reliability using synthetic datasets.
- Recommend appropriate governance, mitigation, and monitoring measures.

## 4. Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows four major areas of investigation.

### 4.1 Ethical Risk Assessment

Eight key ethical risks were identified:

- E01 – Biometric Privacy Violations and Surveillance
- E02 – Algorithmic Bias and Discrimination
- E03 – Insufficient Transparency and Explainability
- E04 – False Positives and False Negatives
- E05 – Digital Exclusion and Unequal Access
- E06 – Data Security and Unauthorised Access
- E07 – Insufficient Human Oversight and Accountability
- E08 – Operational Reliability and Infrastructure Disruption

Each risk was evaluated using preliminary likelihood and impact scores.

### 4.2 NIST AI Risk Management Framework

The assessment applies the four core functions of NIST AI RMF 1.0:

- **GOVERN:** Establish AI policies, responsibilities, accountability, and oversight.
- **MAP:** Identify system purposes, stakeholders, operating environments, and potential impacts.
- **MEASURE:** Evaluate AI risks through fairness, explainability, performance, and reliability testing.
- **MANAGE:** Prioritise risks and recommend mitigation, monitoring, and corrective actions.

### 4.3 Legal and Regulatory Assessment

The assessment considers:

- Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.
- European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (2024).
- UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021).
- OECD AI Principles.

The assessment distinguishes binding legal requirements from voluntary international ethical frameworks.

### 4.4 Practical AI Evaluation

A Python-based evaluation script generates synthetic datasets and performs experiments covering:

- Identity verification performance and subgroup fairness.
- Fraud detection performance and subgroup analysis.
- Credit assessment fairness and approval differences.
- Credit model feature importance using permutation importance.
- Model reliability under controlled input perturbations.

## 5. Synthetic Data and Evaluation

All datasets used in this project are synthetically generated for educational and experimental purposes.

The evaluation uses:

| Component | Dataset Size | Evaluation Method |
|---|---:|---|
| Identity Verification | 1,000 records | Simulated matching score and threshold |
| Fraud Detection | 2,000 transactions | Logistic Regression |
| Credit Risk Assessment | 1,000 applicants | Logistic Regression |

The experiments use a random seed of 42 and a 75% training / 25% testing split for the trained fraud and credit models.

The identity verification experiment uses a simulated score-based method rather than a trained biometric recognition model.

### Key Evaluation Findings

The synthetic evaluation produced the following results:

| Evaluation | Result |
|---|---:|
| Identity verification accuracy | 87.00% |
| Identity decision change rate | 14.80% |
| Fraud detection accuracy | 89.20% |
| Fraud detection recall | 15.69% |
| Fraud false negative rate | 84.31% |
| Female–male credit approval difference | 4.16 percentage points |
| Regional credit approval difference | 15.81 percentage points |

These findings highlight areas requiring further investigation, particularly fraud detection recall, identity verification stability, and credit approval disparities.

**Important:** These results represent synthetic experimental performance only. They must not be interpreted as evidence of actual production performance, real-world discrimination, or legal compliance.

## 9. Limitations

This project has several limitations:

1. All datasets and labels are synthetic.
2. Identity verification uses simulated matching scores rather than a real biometric recognition model.
3. Fraud and credit models are trained using generated data.
4. Fraud detection response times are simulated and do not represent production infrastructure.
5. Fairness differences are diagnostic findings and do not independently prove discrimination.
6. Permutation importance indicates model reliance on features but does not establish causation.
7. Controlled perturbation tests do not establish real-world reliability.
8. Synthetic evaluation results cannot establish production accuracy, operational readiness, or legal compliance.

Further testing with appropriately governed, representative data and independent technical and legal reviews would be necessary before real-world deployment.

## 10. Ethical and Data Protection Statement

No actual customer records, personal financial information, or real biometric data are intentionally used in this assessment.

The project uses synthetic data to demonstrate AI evaluation methods while considering privacy, fairness, transparency, security, accountability, and responsible AI governance.
