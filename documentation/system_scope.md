
# SecureFinance Africa – AI System Scope

## 1. Organisation Overview

SecureFinance Africa is a fictional AI-powered financial services
platform. The organisation plans to deploy its AI-powered services
in Nigeria, with possible expansion into other international markets.

The platform uses artificial intelligence and machine learning to
support customer identity verification, financial fraud detection,
and automated financial risk assessment.

This assessment evaluates the ethical, technical, governance, and
legal implications of deploying such a platform within the African
financial services environment.

## 2. Intended Deployment and Geographic Scope

Primary deployment country: Nigeria.

Potential future expansion:
- Ghana
- Kenya
- South Africa
- Other African countries
- International markets, potentially including the European Union

The initial assessment focuses on Nigeria. Other jurisdictions are
considered only where relevant to possible future expansion or
international regulatory exposure.

## 3. AI System Functions

### 3.1 Customer Identity Verification and KYC

The proposed identity verification component uses AI and automated
document-processing technologies to support customer onboarding
and identity validation.

Potential capabilities include:

- Government database verification, including checks against
  national identity registries such as Nigeria's National
  Identification Number (NIN).
- Biometric verification using facial matching and selfie
  comparisons.
- Liveness detection to identify possible spoofing attempts.
- Document authentication through automated scanning,
  information extraction, and tampering detection.
- Screening against relevant sanctions, politically exposed
  persons, and other regulatory watchlists.

The purpose is to reduce identity fraud, improve onboarding
efficiency, and support Know Your Customer (KYC) requirements.

These capabilities are proposed assessment features. The precise
government integrations, biometric vendors, and verification
procedures used by SecureFinance Africa have not been specified.

### 3.2 AI-Powered Fraud Detection

The proposed fraud detection component analyses financial activity
to identify suspicious transactions and unusual customer behaviour.

Potential capabilities include:

- Real-time transaction monitoring.
- Behavioural profiling and anomaly detection.
- Identification of unusual spending and transfer patterns.
- Cross-channel monitoring across mobile wallets, cards, and
  online banking.
- Detection of potentially coordinated fraud involving related
  accounts, devices, and phone numbers.
- Reduction of false-positive fraud alerts.

Potential technologies include:

- Supervised machine-learning models for known fraud patterns.
- Unsupervised learning and anomaly-detection techniques.
- Gradient-boosted decision trees.
- Isolation Forest algorithms.
- Graph-based machine-learning techniques.

Automated model monitoring and feedback processes may be used
to identify changes in fraud patterns and support model updates.

The actual algorithms, model configurations, training datasets,
and transaction volumes have not been specified.

### 3.3 Financial Risk Assessment and Alternative Credit Scoring

The proposed financial risk assessment component evaluates
customer or business financial risk to support lending and
financial decision-making.

Potential data sources include:

- Traditional financial and credit histories.
- Mobile money transaction records.
- Airtime purchase and top-up histories.
- Utility payment histories.
- Relevant financial transaction patterns.
- Other permitted alternative financial data.

The system may use these data sources to assess individuals
and small or medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) that have limited
or no conventional credit histories.

Potential applications include:

- Alternative credit scoring.
- Automated underwriting support.
- Financial risk classification.
- Identification of potential repayment difficulties.
- Early-warning financial distress indicators.

The assessment will consider the risk of unfairly excluding
customers who lack conventional banking or credit histories.

The exact scoring model, approval thresholds, and final
decision-making arrangements have not been specified.

## 6. AI Decision-Making and Automation

The proposed platform may automate or support:

- Identity verification results.
- Suspicious transaction detection.
- Fraud-risk alerts.
- Customer risk classifications.
- Creditworthiness assessments.
- Financial risk recommendations.

However, the degree of automation is not specified in the
case study.

For this assessment, human oversight will be evaluated as a
necessary governance safeguard, particularly for decisions
that may significantly affect customers.

High-impact actions, such as account restrictions, rejection
of financial applications, or other adverse decisions, should
have appropriate review, escalation, and appeal mechanisms.

## 7. Human Oversight

Human oversight should be considered throughout the AI lifecycle.

Relevant responsibilities include:

- Reviewing disputed identity verification results.
- Investigating suspicious transaction alerts.
- Reviewing potentially incorrect fraud classifications.
- Assessing disputed credit-risk decisions.
- Investigating potential bias and discrimination.
- Approving significant model changes.
- Handling customer complaints and appeals.

The current case study does not specify SecureFinance Africa's
actual human oversight structure. The assessment will therefore
evaluate the controls that should be implemented.

## 8. African Financial Services Context

The assessment considers regional challenges that may affect
the reliability and fairness of AI systems.

These include:

- Limited formal credit histories among some customers.
- Differences in access to digital financial services.
- Inconsistent digitisation of identity and financial records.
- Language and demographic diversity.
- Unequal internet connectivity and electricity availability.
- Variations in financial infrastructure across countries.
- Differences in national regulatory requirements.
- Limited availability of specialised AI governance expertise.

These factors may affect data quality, model performance,
accessibility, fairness, and operational resilience.

## 9. Assumptions and Limitations

The following assumptions are used to establish the assessment
scope:

1. SecureFinance Africa is a fictional organisation.
2. Nigeria is the initial intended deployment country.
3. The platform is assumed to use AI for identity verification,
   fraud detection, and financial risk assessment.
4. The additional technical capabilities in this scope are
   proposed capabilities based on the supplied background
   resource, not independently verified company features.
5. No actual customer records or production datasets are
   available for this assessment.
6. Any experimental data used will be synthetic.
7. The specific AI models, vendors, datasets, infrastructure,
   and deployment architecture are not known.
8. The actual degree of automated decision-making is not known.
9. Human oversight arrangements have not been confirmed.
10. Future expansion into other countries is a possibility,
    not a confirmed deployment plan.

The assessment will distinguish documented facts, proposed
system capabilities, assumptions, and recommendations.
